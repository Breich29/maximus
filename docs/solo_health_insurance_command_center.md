## 1. The Real Problem: Access, Not Attach

The 0.3% health insurance rate for Gusto Solo customers is not an attach rate problem. It is an access problem.

Employer-sponsored health insurance covers ~160M Americans — and it's the dominant system because it's subsidized, tax-advantaged, and priced through risk pools that make coverage genuinely affordable. Solos are structurally excluded from it. Federal law defines a small group employer as one with at least one non-owner W-2 employee, which means a solo operating as a single-member LLC cannot qualify. They're left with the ACA individual market: more expensive, higher deductibles, and unsubsidized for anyone with a decent income.

The 0.3% isn't apathy. It's a rational response to a system that prices solos out. You don't fix that by optimizing a referral link — you fix it by unlocking access that doesn't currently exist.

**The opportunity reframe:** Gusto is not trying to improve a conversion metric. Gusto is positioned to give solos the same health insurance access that 160M W-2 employees take for granted. That is a category-defining product moment, not a funnel problem.

### The Access Gap
- ~30M self-employed Americans in the U.S.
- No access to employer-sponsored small group rates
- Forced into ACA individual market: a healthy 35-year-old can pay $400–600/month with a $5K+ deductible
- The same person employed at a 10-person company pays ~$150–250/month with a $1–2K deductible
- That delta — ~$250–350/month — is the access gap Gusto can close

## 2. Why Gusto Is Uniquely Positioned

Most companies trying to solve solo health insurance are starting cold. Gusto is not.

**Gusto already has:**
- 3,000–4,000 new solo customers per month — scale no boutique solo PEO can approach
- Payroll data = verified income visibility, which is the hardest input to get in insurance underwriting
- Entity formation (SYB) — the legal prerequisite for ICHRA and MEWA paths
- Existing broker relationships — carrier partnerships don't start from zero
- Brand trust — solos already trust Gusto with their payroll and taxes

**The natural product sequence:**
Entity formation (SYB) → ICHRA or PEO eligibility → pre-tax health spend → integrated into allocation/compensation modeling → Gusto as the financial OS for solo business owners

Health insurance is not a standalone product to bolt on. It is the capstone of a bundle that Gusto is already 70% of the way toward — because entity management and payroll are already in the platform. No competitor can offer this sequence. Lettuce is PEO-only. Stride is marketplace-only. Neither can touch the full financial picture.

### A Note on Lettuce

Lettuce is a useful proof point but not a benchmark. They bring in ~100 new customers per month — roughly 30–40x fewer than Gusto's monthly solo volume. Their 50% PEO adoption rate reflects a boutique, high-touch model operating at a scale where hand-holding every customer is possible. It validates that solos *want* this benefit when real access is provided. It does not tell us what's achievable at Gusto's velocity and self-serve model.

At Gusto's scale, even 3–5% adoption of a real health insurance product (not a referral link) means 100–200 solos per month gaining access to affordable coverage for the first time. That compounds fast and has genuine social impact — not just a revenue line.

## 3. Coverage Landscape for Solos

Health insurance access pathways for self-employed/solo workers:

### A. ACA Marketplace Plans (Individual Market)
- Standard on-exchange plans via Healthcare.gov; subsidy-eligible based on income
- The current default for solos — expensive and high-deductible for most
- **Gusto angle:** Could offer an embedded enrollment experience rather than a referral, reducing friction without requiring a new product structure

### B. ICHRA (Individual Coverage HRA)
- Employer (the solo's own business entity) contributes pre-tax dollars to the individual; employee uses funds to buy any ACA plan
- Solo can effectively "pay themselves" a health benefit with pre-tax business dollars
- Requires the solo to have a business entity (LLC, S-corp, etc.) — **strong fit with SYB/Entity Management**
- **Regulatory:** Employer-sponsored under ERISA; not subject to ACA individual mandate
- **Gusto angle:** Highly strategic — bridges Gusto Solo + SYB + entity management in one flow. The tax benefit alone changes the effective cost of ACA coverage meaningfully.

### C. Health Sharing Ministries / Communities
- Not insurance — members crowd-fund each other's medical bills
- Lower monthly cost (~$175–350/mo for individuals) but no ACA protections
- Pre-existing conditions often excluded or limited; not all services covered
- **Regulatory:** Not regulated as insurance; state-by-state legal treatment varies
- **Gusto angle:** Addresses the cost objection but carries brand and coverage risk; needs compliance review before any Gusto association

### D. MEWA (Multiple Employer Welfare Arrangement)

#### What Is a MEWA?
A MEWA is a benefits arrangement where multiple employers pool together to provide welfare benefits (health, dental, vision, life) to their employees. Governed jointly by federal ERISA and state insurance law.

#### How It Works for Solos
- A solo's single-member LLC qualifies as an "employer" — can join a MEWA as a member employer
- The MEWA pools risk across many small employers, creating group-like economics for solos
- Premiums are typically lower than individual ACA plans (no individual rate-up)
- Can offer richer benefits than individual ACA plans in some cases

#### Pros
- Group economics for solo/small employers — this is the access unlock
- Employer tax deduction on premiums
- More plan flexibility than ACA exchange plans
- Strong fit for association-like platforms (Gusto Solo as aggregator)

#### Cons / Risks
- **Regulatory complexity:** Subject to both ERISA (federal) and state insurance law — state approval often required
- **Historical fraud/underfunding risk:** MEWAs have a documented history of insolvency; DOL actively monitors
- **Not ACA-compliant:** MEWA plans are not required to cover all ACA essential health benefits
- **State-by-state approval:** Not available in all states; multi-state MEWAs require individual state filings

#### Gusto Viability Assessment
**Medium-term consideration, not near-term.** Gusto would need to sponsor or white-label a MEWA, which requires insurance/compliance infrastructure Gusto doesn't have today. More realistic near-term path: partner with an existing MEWA operator designed for freelancers/solos.

### E. Short-Term / Non-ACA Plans
- Cheaper but limited coverage; not recommended as a primary path given regulatory headwinds
- **Gusto angle:** Not recommended

## 5. Strategic Options

All options below should be evaluated through the lens of: *does this actually close the access gap?* A referral link does not. An embedded marketplace improves UX but doesn't change the underlying cost structure. The highest-value paths are those that move solos from individual-market pricing to group-market pricing.

### Option A: Deepen Stride Integration
- Keep Stride as partner; negotiate embedded in-product enrollment (not just referral link)
- Improves the UX significantly but does not change the access problem — solos still get individual-market ACA pricing
- **Target:** Get Stride enrollment inside Gusto Solo onboarding flow — reduce friction
- **Ceiling:** Still ACA individual market. Better attach, same structural pricing disadvantage.

### Option B: ICHRA + HealthSherpa
- Build an ICHRA flow (solo's LLC contributes pre-tax to individual plan)
- Use HealthSherpa's Off-Exchange API to power enrollment
- **Why this matters for access:** ICHRA converts health insurance from a personal expense to a pre-tax business deduction — effectively reducing the real cost of ACA coverage by 20–37% depending on the solo's tax bracket. Not quite group-market pricing, but closes part of the gap without the regulatory complexity of PEO.
- **Strategic fit:** Requires entity formation (SYB synergy) — creates a natural upsell sequence
- **Risk:** Build complexity; HealthSherpa API requires review/approval
- **Timing:** Medium-term (not Q3 2026)

### Option C: Curative / Solo PEO
- Partner with Curative to offer solo PEO health coverage (the Lettuce model)
- **Why this matters for access:** PEO is the most direct path to group-market pricing for solos. This is the access unlock, not just a distribution improvement.
- **Lettuce context:** Lettuce proved this works at small scale (~100/month). Gusto's 3–4K/month volume would make this the largest solo health insurance program in the country if executed well.
- **Risk:** PEO structure adds co-employment complexity; Curative not in all 50 states; economics unclear
- **Timing:** Stephanie + Vivian are already in this path — let it develop

### Option D: HBG Solo Captive (Gusto-Branded, Lower Cost)
- Partner with HBG Solo to offer Gusto-branded self-insured health plans at lower cost than ACA marketplace
- **Access impact:** Pooled risk model moves solos off individual-market pricing. If premiums are materially lower (e.g., $300/mo vs. $500/mo on ACA), the access gap closes meaningfully without the regulatory complexity of a PEO.
- **Margin:** Highest of any option (20–25%+) if claims experience holds — Gusto earns underwriting margin, not just a referral or commission
- **Risk:** Insurance risk is real — if the solo risk pool has worse-than-expected claims, economics deteriorate. Capital-intensive. State licensing TBD.
- **Decision gate:** Only pursue if HBG Solo pricing is verifiably ≥20% below ACA individual market AND claims risk structure is acceptable
- **Timing:** In pipeline — validate pricing and risk structure before committing

### Option E: Health-Sharing as a "Starter" Option
- Add CrowdHealth (or similar) as a low-cost option alongside ACA plans
- Addresses the price objection but does not solve the access problem — health sharing is not insurance and solos retain all risk
- **Risk:** Regulatory, brand, and coverage gaps — needs compliance review first
- **Timing:** Only after Option A or C has a clearer path; position as "alternative" not "solution"

## 6. Recommended Path (Draft)

**The sequencing principle:** Start with what improves access fastest, layer in what closes the structural gap over time.

**Near-term (Q3 2026):** Let Curative exploration run (Stephanie + Vivian). If economics work, Option C is the right primary path — it's the only option that actually moves solos from individual-market to group-market pricing, which is the real access unlock.

**Parallel:** Push Stride to deepen integration (Option A) — not because it solves the access problem, but because it will immediately improve the experience for solos who end up on ACA plans regardless, and it's low-cost to pursue alongside Curative.

**Medium-term:** Build ICHRA flow tied to SYB/entity management (Option B). The pre-tax benefit closes a meaningful portion of the access gap for solos who have entities. It also creates a natural product sequence: form entity → unlock ICHRA → Gusto handles payroll + health + taxes in one place.

**Parallel — validate HBG Solo:** Get actual pricing and risk structure from HBG Solo. If premiums are ≥20% below ACA individual market and claims risk is manageable, this becomes the recommended primary path over Curative — it's the highest-margin option and doesn't require PEO co-employment complexity. If pricing doesn't hold up, deprioritize.

**Longer-term:** If ICHRA + entity formation becomes a Gusto product, evaluate HealthSherpa API as the enrollment layer. This is the highest-upside option strategically — it positions Gusto as the financial OS for solo business owners, not just a payroll tool.

**Defer:** CrowdHealth and MEWA paths until compliance review is complete and primary path is clearer.
